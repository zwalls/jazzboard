- Date: 2026-09-03

## Result

EXP-0034 is the first accepted dense-routing completion in this experiment
sequence. The isolated author created all nine required entities and all nine
directed relationships, kept the fixed frame and legend, invented no protocols,
providers, or ownership boundaries, and committed one first-class Diagram. The
final authoritative analyzer found zero failures and zero warnings. Two fresh,
blinded `gpt-5.6-sol` reviewers independently inspected only sanitized semantic
state and the exported PNG; both passed facts, routing, and anti-gaming with zero
blocking geometry violations. No adjudication was required.

The new recovery branch produced the intended behavioral change. The author's
first draft had 11 failures and seven warnings. After revising its own node
geometry, the draft had 17 failures and four warnings. The author then compared
two route alternatives. Both failed: `perimeter_elbows` had three failures and
`right_lanes` had one. Instead of stopping, committing, or rationalizing the
remaining defect as EXP-0033 did, the author followed the explicit all-fail next
step and authored a materially different `separated_service_a_ports`
alternative. That candidate had zero failures and one warning. The author chose
and applied it itself, inspected exact draft pixels, moved the remaining event
route inside the fixed frame, reached zero findings, and committed.

Jazzboard did not generate coordinates, compose the diagram, rank or select a
candidate, apply a route, or decide whether a finding matched the user's intent.
The intervention supplied evidence and a truthful recovery branch; the author
retained the creative and semantic decisions.

## Speed and traffic

Author wall time was 303,113 ms. Compared with EXP-0032, this accepted result was
17,174 ms faster (-5.36%), used 13 rather than 14 WebMCP business calls, used four
rather than seven accepted draft transactions, and returned 25.90% fewer bytes
across accepted draft transactions. EXP-0032 failed both blinded routing and
anti-gaming review.

Compared with EXP-0031, the accepted result was 250,796 ms faster (-45.28%), used
13 rather than 24 business calls, used four rather than 16 draft transactions,
and returned 57.22% fewer draft-response bytes. EXP-0031 reached zero
deterministic failures but retained a crossing that both blinded reviewers found
blocking. EXP-0034 eliminated that final crossing and passed both reviewers.

EXP-0034 took 15,993 ms longer (+5.57%) than EXP-0033, which stopped with an
uncommitted failed draft. That difference is not a like-for-like accepted-speed
regression: the extra work produced and verified the artifact that EXP-0033 did
not complete.

Of EXP-0034's 303.0 seconds of accounted wall time, 48.6 seconds were host tool
execution and 254.4 seconds were model/coordination time. Inspection accounted
for 105.6 seconds, including three semantic inspections and three exact pixel
captures. Initial authoring accounted for 115.3 seconds. This identifies useful
future speed targets without weakening the quality gate: reduce discovery and
inspection round trips, but retain one exact uncommitted pixel review and one
authoritative completion check.

## Scientific interpretation

This result is strong evidence that the all-fail recovery message fixes the
specific failure mode observed in EXP-0033. It is not yet a stable percentage
estimate for the population. The same frozen task should be replicated before
claiming an effect size, and later generalized to a different dense architecture
fixture. The current defensible statement is narrower: one prospective,
isolated attempt used the new branch exactly as intended and converted the prior
terminal behavior into the sequence's first unanimous quality pass while also
beating the two preceding committed attempts on latency and traffic.

The result also suggests where the next product improvement should focus. The
author still spent substantial time acquiring browser context, reading the
quickstart, and performing three inspection cycles. A future intervention should
make exact inspection cheaper to invoke or consolidate its compact receipt, not
remove pixel evidence or allow Jazzboard to choose composition on the author's
behalf.

## Protocol notes

The prompt, fixture, model, reasoning level, and projectless isolation were
unchanged from EXP-0032 and EXP-0033. The author had no repository access,
private API access, prepared coordinates, evaluator context, or paired result.
It used browser-exposed WebMCP for every canvas operation.

Each reviewer ran in a fresh projectless context and received only the frozen
public requirement, frozen rubric, sanitized semantic state, and final PNG. No
reviewer received the room credential, author transcript, product condition,
paired result, or repository access.

Exact tokens and resolved model snapshot were unobservable. Subscription usage
was 95% after the attempt and both reviews; no usage-limit interruption occurred
and no reset credit was consumed.
